# Privacy under Law 25: what a Québec product owes the people in its data

**As of:** 2026-10-26

**Paths:** `privacy/`, `*privacy*`, `*Privacy*`, `consent/`, `*consent*`, `*Consent*`

**Stack:** `better-auth`, `next-auth`, `@auth/*`, `lucia`, `passport`

**If today is more than 180 days past `**As of:**`, say so in one line before relying on a section, a delay or a threshold, and keep going.** Re-check a dated fact against its source before it decides anything.

**This is not legal advice.** The legal reading of what applies to this business, and every policy text, belong to the project and its counsel; this module names the obligations an implementation must make possible, and where to check them. **A project skill on the same topic wins** where the two disagree. How an account deletion ends sessions and grants belongs to `esq:auth-sessions`; what app stores require of account deletion to `esq:store-review`; the language a notice is written in to `esq:i18n-fr-en`. This module owns why data is held, for how long, where it goes, and what the person and the regulator can ask for.

Privacy failures do not crash: the data is collected, kept and shipped abroad silently, and the cost arrives with an incident, a complaint or an access request nobody can answer in 30 days. Each rule below says what the code must be able to show.

**Sources and how far they were checked.** The rules follow the Commission d'accès à l'information (CAI)'s guidance pages for businesses and the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada (OPC), read on 2026-10-26. The Act is the *Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector*, [CQLR c. P-39.1](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1). **The section numbers below are as the Act is commonly cited: LégisQuébec refused automated reads on 2026-10-26, so none was re-read there.** Confirm a section on LégisQuébec before quoting it.

## Someone is in charge, by name

- **The person in charge of protecting personal information is, by default, the person with the highest authority in the business; the role can be delegated in writing, and their title and contact details are published on the website** (s. 3.1; [CAI, responsable](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/protection-renseignements-personnels/information-entreprises-privees/responsable-protection-renseignements-personnels-entreprise), checked 2026-10-26). The business also publishes its governance rules and, for a product that collects by technological means, a privacy policy in clear terms (ss. 3.2, 8.2). *For the code:* the privacy page and the footer link to it exist in both languages, and the contact is a role address that survives staff changes. *Check:* the published page names a title and a working address.

## An assessment before a new system or a transfer out of Québec

- **A privacy impact assessment (EFVP) precedes any project to acquire, develop or overhaul a system that handles personal information** (s. 3.3), **and any communication of personal information outside Québec, which also needs a written agreement and proceeds only if the information will be adequately protected** (s. 17; [CAI, utilisation et communication](https://cai.gouv.qc.ca/protection-renseignements-personnels/information-entreprises-privees/utilisation-communication-renseignements-personnels), checked 2026-10-26). The CAI publishes a [guide](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/uploads/pdfs/CAI_GU_EFVP.pdf) and a report template. *Why it bites in code:* a new SaaS dependency (hosting, email, analytics, error tracking, an AI API) hosted in Ontario or the United States is a communication outside Québec, added in a single `npm install` and an environment variable. *Fix:* keep a register of every provider that receives personal information, with its region; a change that adds one or moves one names the assessment it needs in the plan, and the user decides. *Check:* grep the change for new outbound hosts and SDKs and compare them with the register.

## Consent for each purpose

- **Consent is manifest, free and enlightened, given for specific purposes, requested separately for each purpose in clear and simple terms, and valid only as long as needed for them; sensitive information (medical, biometric, financial or otherwise intimate) needs express consent** (ss. 12, 14; [CAI, consentement](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/protection-renseignements-personnels/information-entreprises-privees/consentement-personnes-entreprises), checked 2026-10-26). A person under 14 consents through a parent or tutor (s. 4.1). *Why:* one checkbox for "terms, privacy and marketing" is not consent to marketing; information collected for one purpose and reused for another (analytics, training a model, a newsletter) needs consent again unless an exception applies, and the CAI reads the exceptions narrowly. *Fix:* store each consent as its own record (purpose, version of the text, timestamp, how it was given), unchecked by default, withdrawable from the account, and checked by the code before each use. *Check:* create an account without ticking the optional boxes and confirm no marketing email, no optional tracking and no secondary use happens.

## Private by default

- **A technological product offered to the public ships its privacy settings at the highest level of confidentiality, without the person acting; cookies are excluded from that rule** (s. 9.1; the Act's text was not read, see Sources). **Functions that identify, locate or profile the person are off until the person turns them on, and the person is told they exist** (s. 8.1). *Fix:* a profile is private, location is off, optional analytics and third-party trackers do not load before a choice, and the cookie banner refuses as easily as it accepts. *Check:* open the app in a fresh profile, accept nothing, and see in the network panel that no optional tracker loads.

## Kept only as long as needed, then destroyed or anonymised

- **Once the purposes are achieved, personal information is destroyed or anonymised, unless a law sets a retention period; anonymised means it is reasonable to expect that it irreversibly no longer identifies the person, under generally accepted practices and the government's regulation** (s. 23; [CAI, conservation et destruction](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/protection-renseignements-personnels/information-entreprises-privees/conservation-destruction-renseignements-personnels), checked 2026-10-26). Removing the name and email is de-identification, and the data stays personal information; the CAI considers genetic, biometric and location data impossible to anonymise adequately. *Why:* "we keep everything" is the default of every database, every backup and every log store. *Fix:* a retention schedule per kind of data (account, invoices, logs, backups, support tickets), with the legal basis of each fixed period recorded by the project; a scheduled job that deletes or anonymises what has expired (`esq:background-jobs` for running it safely); backups that age out on the same schedule. *Check:* the schedule exists, the job runs in staging, and a record past its period is gone from the database and from the newest backup.

## Incidents: a register for all, a notice for the serious

- **On reason to believe a confidentiality incident occurred (unauthorised access, use or communication, or loss), the business takes reasonable measures to reduce the risk; it promptly notifies the CAI and the people concerned when the incident presents a risk of serious injury; and it records every incident, serious or not, in a register kept at least five years and given to the CAI on request** (ss. 3.5 to 3.8 and the regulation on confidentiality incidents; [CAI, incidents](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/protection-renseignements-personnels/information-entreprises-privees/incidents-confidentialite-mesures-securite-entreprises), checked 2026-10-26). Where PIPEDA also applies, a breach creating a real risk of significant harm is reported to the OPC and to the individuals as soon as feasible, and every breach is recorded and kept two years ([OPC, breaches](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/business-privacy/breaches-and-safeguards/privacy-breaches-at-your-business/gd_pb_201810/), checked 2026-10-26). Whether PIPEDA applies to the business is the project's legal reading. *For the code:* logs and access records detailed enough to say who saw what, when and how many people are affected; that question cannot be answered after the fact from logs that were never written. *Check:* for a sample record, the system can list who accessed it in the last month.

## Access, rectification and portability within 30 days

- **A person can ask for their information and its correction, and the business answers within 30 days of receipt; silence at the deadline counts as a refusal. Computerised information collected from the person is also provided, on request, in a structured, commonly used technological format, to them or to a body they name** (ss. 27, 32; [CAI, accès](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/protection-renseignements-personnels/citoyens-protection-renseignements-personnels/acces-et-protection-renseignements-personnels), checked 2026-10-26). Information the business inferred or created is outside portability. *Why:* data spread across the database, the email provider, the payment processor and the analytics tool cannot be gathered by hand in 30 days for every request. *Fix:* one export per person (JSON or CSV) that walks every table and provider holding their data, built from the same provider register as the assessments above, and a correction path for each field the person can see. *Check:* request the export for a test account and find in it each kind of data the register says is held.
