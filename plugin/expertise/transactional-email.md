# Transactional email: delivered, readable, and safe to click

**As of:** 2026-10-28

**Paths:** `email/`, `emails/`, `mail/`, `mailer/`, `*mailer*`, `*Mailer*`, `*.mjml`

**Stack:** `nodemailer`, `resend`, `postmark`, `@sendgrid/*`, `mailgun.js`, `@aws-sdk/client-ses`, `@aws-sdk/client-sesv2`, `@react-email/*`, `react-email`, `mjml`

**If today is more than 180 days past `**As of:**`, say so in one line before relying on a mailbox provider's threshold or a statute's wording, and keep going.** Re-check a dated fact against its source before it decides anything.

**A project skill on the same topic wins** where the two disagree. Sending from a queue, with retries that never send twice, belongs to `esq:background-jobs`; the language a message is written in, from the recipient's stored choice, to `esq:i18n-fr-en`; what a password reset does to the account's other sessions to `esq:auth-sessions`; whether the provider's region needs a privacy assessment to `esq:privacy-loi25`. This module owns whether mail arrives, how it renders, and what its links let a reader do.

An email bug never shows in the developer's own inbox: their domain is warm, their client renders HTML, and they click the link once. It shows as resets landing in spam at one provider, a magic link already "used" when the user clicks it, or an unreadable white-on-white message in dark mode. Each rule below says how to see it first.

**These rules are documented, not yet tested in a shipped project.** They follow Google's and Yahoo's sender guidelines, the DMARC RFCs and Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation as consolidated, checked on the date above, and state the general rule first; provider specifics are conditional. Treat each as a hypothesis to prove on the first message that relies on it. **The CASL lines state the rule and its source; whether a given message is commercial is the project's call, with its own counsel. They are not legal advice.**

## The domain proves it sent the mail

- **SPF and DKIM pass, and DMARC passes through alignment with the visible `From:` domain.** *Why:* Gmail requires SPF or DKIM of every sender, and of anyone sending more than 5 000 messages a day to Gmail all three, with the `From:` domain aligned with the SPF or the DKIM domain and a DMARC policy (`p=none` accepted), DKIM keys of at least 1024 bits, valid forward and reverse DNS and TLS ([Gmail sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126), in effect since 2024-02-01, checked 2026-10-28); Yahoo requires the same of bulk senders ([Yahoo sender requirements](https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/), checked 2026-10-28). A provider's default setup often signs DKIM with *its* domain and passes SPF on *its* bounce domain, so both pass and neither aligns. *Fix:* a custom DKIM key on your domain and a custom return-path (bounce) domain under it, per the provider's domain-authentication guide; a DMARC record with `rua` reports, starting at `p=none` and tightening once reports are clean. DMARC is now RFC 9989, a 2026 Proposed Standard that obsoletes RFC 7489: read a provider's tag advice against it ([RFC 9989](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9989), checked 2026-10-28). *Check:* send to a Gmail address, open "Show original", and read `Authentication-Results`: `spf=pass`, `dkim=pass` with `header.d=` your domain, `dmarc=pass`.
- **SPF stays under ten DNS lookups.** *Why:* each `include:` costs lookups, and past ten SPF returns `permerror`, which counts as a fail; adding one more provider is how it breaks. *Check:* an SPF checker on the record after every provider change.
- **Mail is sent from a subdomain, and transactional mail apart from marketing.** *Why:* reputation is earned per domain and per IP; a newsletter that draws complaints drags down the password resets sent from the same domain and stream. *Fix:* `mail.example.com` (or `notify.`) for transactional mail and another subdomain or the provider's separate stream for marketing, each with its own DKIM and return-path. *Check:* the two kinds of message show different `header.d=` and return-path domains.

## The bulk-sender rules, dated

- **Marketing and subscribed mail carries one-click unsubscribe, honoured within two days, and spam complaints stay below 0.3 %.** *Why:* Gmail and Yahoo both require `List-Unsubscribe` with one-click (RFC 8058, the `List-Unsubscribe-Post` header) on marketing and subscribed messages from bulk senders, and a spam rate below 0.3 %; Yahoo asks that unsubscribes be honoured within 2 days, and Gmail's monitoring advice is to stay under 0.10 % (sources above, checked 2026-10-28). Transactional messages (a receipt, a reset) are not marketing, but count towards the volume and the spam rate. *Fix:* the provider's unsubscribe headers on every marketing stream, wired to the suppression list below; Postmaster Tools on the sending domain. *Check:* a marketing message's raw headers show both `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`.

## Bounces and complaints stop the sending

- **A hard bounce or a complaint suppresses the address, through the provider's webhook.** *Why:* sending again to an address that bounced or complained is the fastest way to lose reputation, and the provider's dashboard is not where the app decides whom to send to. *Fix:* subscribe to bounce and complaint events, verify the webhook's signature, and record a suppression the app checks before sending; a soft bounce retries and suppresses after repeats. Retrying the send itself is `esq:background-jobs`'s. *Check:* send to the provider's bounce and complaint simulator addresses and see the suppression row, then see the next send skipped.

## Links that a scanner cannot spend

- **A token in a link is single-use, short-lived, random and stored only as a hash.** *Why:* the link sits in an inbox, a forwarded thread and a provider's logs; a token that lasts days or is stored in clear lets anyone who reads it, or the database, take the account. *Fix:* at least 128 random bits, a hash (SHA-256 suffices for random tokens) stored with an expiry measured in minutes for a reset or a magic link, the row consumed on use, and older tokens of the same purpose invalidated when a new one is issued. What the reset then does to the account's sessions is `esq:auth-sessions`'s. *Check:* use a link twice and wait past its expiry; both later attempts fail, and the database holds no clear token.
- **Opening the link changes nothing; a button on the page confirms it by `POST`.** *Why:* corporate link scanners and some mail clients fetch every URL in a message before the user sees it; a `GET` that consumes the token spends it, so the user finds "link already used", and a `GET` that signs in signs in the scanner. *Fix:* the `GET` renders a page with one button that `POST`s the token; only the `POST` consumes it. *Check:* `curl` the link (as a scanner would), then open it in the browser; the browser flow still succeeds.

## A message every client can read

- **Every message has a plain-text part, and its HTML survives dark mode.** *Why:* a message with HTML only scores worse with spam filters and is unreadable in text clients; clients that invert colours in dark mode turn a transparent black logo invisible and dark grey text onto dark grey. *Fix:* the template tool's text output (`render(…, { plainText: true })` with React Email, or a hand-written text part with nodemailer's `text`), explicit background colours on containers, a logo with a light outline or a solid background, and links whose text says where they go. *Check:* view each template in the provider's or a client-preview tool's dark mode, and read the text part alone.

## CASL: which messages need consent

- **Under CASL, a message whose only purpose is a transaction or an existing relationship needs no consent, but still carries the sender's identity, contact details and, for a commercial message, an unsubscribe mechanism.** Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (S.C. 2010, c. 23), [s. 6](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-1.6/page-1.html) (consolidation current to 2026-09-30, checked 2026-10-28): s. 6(1) forbids sending a commercial electronic message without consent (para. (a)) and without the form of s. 6(2) (identification, contact information, an unsubscribe mechanism); s. 6(6) lifts only para. (1)(a), consent, for a message that *solely* confirms a transaction, gives warranty, recall, safety or security information, notifies factual information about an ongoing account or subscription, or delivers a product or an update. The CRTC's guidance pages refused automated reads on 2026-10-28, so its interpretation was not re-read; confirm there. *Why it matters to code:* a receipt with a "you might also like" block is no longer *solely* transactional, and the whole message then needs consent. *Fix:* keep promotional content out of transactional templates; give marketing its own template, stream and consent record. *Check:* each transactional template contains no promotion, and every message names the business and a contact address.
